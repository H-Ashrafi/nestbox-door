Run it before you build it

Wokwi is a free browser simulator that shows real pictures of the boards, lets you drag wires between pins, and runs the actual firmware. There's a ready-made project for this circuit — paste two files in and press play. Same GPIO numbers as the real build, so it maps pin for pin against the diagram above.

A green LED flashes at dawn, a red one mid-afternoon, and a servo swings between them as the door. Turn the knob down past 11.9 V and watch the battery guard park the boxes open. One day passes every thirty seconds.

It proves the logic. It can't catch a swapped VIN and VOUT, a cold joint, or a pull-down on a strapping pin — which is what building in stages is for.

The three red pins

The chip reads D0, D8 and D9 at power-up to decide how to boot. A pull-down on any of them and the board will not start — which is why IN1 and IN2 go to D1 and D3 rather than the obvious D0 and D1.

Read this order twice

Left to right: VOUT · GND · VIN · SHDN. VIN and VOUT are two pads apart with ground between them. Swap them and 12 V goes into the ESP32. Measure VOUT before you connect the XIAO.

The opposite of most clock modules. Wire it from muscle memory and you'll get it backwards — the clock just won't answer.

On the actuator run

Ten metres of figure-8 at 1 mm² drops about a third of a volt at 2 A, which is nothing. The same run in 26 AWG would drop over three volts and cook the wire.

05

Soldering, if you never have

Practise on the spare half of the experimenters board. Twenty throwaway joints and you'll have it.

  1. 350 °C, wet sponge

    Not hotter. Hotter doesn't make it faster, it makes it lift pads.

  2. Tin the tip

    Melt a little solder on, wipe on the sponge. Bright silver. A dull grey tip transfers almost no heat — re-tin every few joints.

  3. Heat the metal, not the solder

    Tip against the pad and the pin at once. Count two.

  4. Feed solder into the joint, not onto the iron

    Touch it to the far side from the tip. It should run and wrap the pin in half a second.

  5. Solder away first, then the iron

    Hold still three seconds. Move it while setting and you get a grainy joint that fails in the coop.

  6. Look at it

    Shiny and cone-shaped, like a tiny volcano hugging the pin. A dull ball sitting on top is a cold joint — reheat until it collapses and flows.

The four ways this goes wrong

  1. Carrying a blob over on the tip. It sticks, it looks finished, it isn't connected.
  2. Ten seconds on one pad. The copper lifts off and that module is scrap. Not flowing in three seconds? Iron away, cool, retry.
  3. Bridging two pads. Easy on 2.54 mm headers. Check every adjacent pair on continuity before powering anything.
  4. No strain relief. A wire flexing at the joint snaps in about a month, usually in the rain.
06

Build it in stages

Never wire the whole thing and then switch on. Add one module, test, add the next — then a fault is always in the thing you just touched.

  1. Headers on all four modules. Nothing else.

    Meter on continuity: every pin to its own pad, and not to its neighbour.

  2. The buck alone

    Battery → fuse → VIN and GND. Meter VOUT to GND: 5.0 V ± 0.1. If it reads 12 V you have VIN and VOUT swapped — and you just saved your ESP32.

  3. Add the XIAO

    VOUT → 5V, GND → GND. Upload Blink.

  4. Add the clock

    3V3, GND, SDA, SCL. Print rtc.now(). If the time counts up, I²C is sound.

  5. Add the divider

    220 kΩ, 33 kΩ, 100 nF. Compare batteryVolts() to the meter, set VBAT_CAL.

  6. Driver and actuator — on the bench, off the panel

    Both pull-downs in. TEST_MODE = true. Ten cycles before it goes near the coop.

  7. Now mount it

    IP65 box under cover, glands down. Battery in a ventilated crate below, on spade terminals.
